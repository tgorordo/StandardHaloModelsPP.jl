Galactic & Solar Ephemerides - the Earth-Frame Distributions

The Earth-frame distributions follow from the galactic-frame ones by a boost with the Earth's galactic velocity as a composition of the Earth-Sun and Sun-(Sag. A⋆) orbits.

StandardHaloModelsPP.jl provides a simple model of that motion in which the Sun's ephemeris follows the conventions of [2], with its peculiar velocity from [8] and its position from [9], and the Earth's orbit follows the conventions of [10, 11].

Ephemerides

The Sun's galactic velocity $\vec{v}_\odot = \vec{v}_\text{LSR} + \vec{v}_\text{PEC}$ is that of the Local Standard of Rest (LSR), the circular orbit about the Galactic Center at the Sun's position, plus the Sun's peculiar velocity relative to it. Schematically (not to scale):

(Fig. gal.asy source)

Galactic-Rectangular coordinates (green, centered in the plane below the Sun) are defined with $\hat{x}$ pointing from the Sun to Sgr A*, $\hat{y}$ along the disk's rotation (clockwise seen from the North Galactic Pole), and $\hat{z}$ to the North Galactic Pole [10] [12, Sec. 10.3.1].

The DM halo velocity distribution is usually expressed in Galactocentric-Spherical coordinates [5]. Its components at the sun are taken along the Galactic-Rectangular axes [1], and at the location of the solar system $(v_r, v_\theta, v_\phi) \equiv (v_x, v_y, v_z)$ (labeled in the figure).

The lab-frame distribution is related by a Galilean boost in that frame [10]: a DM velocity $\vec{v}$ in the lab is $\vec{v} + \vec{v}_E$ in the galaxy; $f_\text{lab}(\vec{v}, t) = f(\vec{v} + \vec{v}_E(t))$.

Velocity of the Sun:

StandardHaloModelsPP.VLSR — Constant

Reference circular speed of the Local Standard of Rest (LSR), used by ephemeris functions when no model is given.

Value: 238.0 ± 1.5 km s^-1

D. Baxter and others, Eur. Phys. J. C 81, 907 (2021); Sec. 3.1.5.

Note

A HaloModel carries its own LSR speed m.VLSR, which by default is its circular speed v₀. The model-aware vE(m, t) and every lab-frame distribution and integral use that parameter when a model is available, not this constant. It equals this one for SHM and SHMerr, but is 233 km/s for SHMpp and 220 km/s for SHMlegacy, as in their defining papers.

The derived reference constants vSUN, T0, VEavg and Tavg use this value whatever the model. T0 in particular is a fixed date.

In most halo models the DM (isothermal) distribution is assumed to be stationary with respect to the galaxy, so it is the motion of the solar system through the galaxy that sets $v_0$, the DM velocity dispersion [5] – i.e. the LSR speed is essentially that parameter of a halo model. Each HaloModel therefore carries its own LSR speed m.VLSR, by default equivalent to its v₀, and ephemeris functions are model-aware; i.e. vE(m, t), the lab-frame distributions and the integrals all use it for their boost to the lab. The constants here are a reference ephemeris, with VLSR from [2] (that of SHM), used whenever no model is given, e.g. by VE(t).

The Earth's orbit is taken as circular, in the ecliptic, i.e. at zeroth order in its eccentricity [10, 11]:

\[\vec{v}_{ES}(t) = V_{ES}\left[\hat\epsilon_1 \cos\omega(t - t_\text{eq}) + \hat\epsilon_2 \sin\omega(t - t_\text{eq})\right],\]

  • $\omega = 2\pi/\text{yr}$ is the orbital angular frequency.
  • $V_{ES} = a\omega \approx 29.79$ km/s, with $a$ = 1 au.
  • $t_\text{eq}$ is the March equinox.
  • $\hat\epsilon_1$ is the direction of the Earth's velocity, and $\hat\epsilon_2$ the Earth → Sun direction, at $t_\text{eq}$.

Both basis vectors are expressed in Galactic-Rectangular coordinates, to first order in the slow precession of the equinoxes (measured from J2000), and evaluated at the epoch of $t_\text{eq}$ [10, Eqs. (13-15)].

The Earth's galactic velocity is then

\[\vec{v}_E(t) = \vec{v}_\odot + \vec{v}_{ES}(t),\]

whose speed peaks at $T_0$ (about June 1st), when $\vec{v}_{ES}$ is most closely aligned with $\vec{v}_\odot$. Times may be given as a DateTime, a Date, a DayOfYear, or a Unitful time, which is read as the time elapsed since $T_0$:

TimeLike = Union{DateTime, Date, DayOfYear, Unitful.Time}

StandardHaloModelsPP.DayOfYear — Type
DayOfYear(d::Real[, year::Integer = Dates.year(T0)])

Day d of year as a time argument: d = 1 is January 1st, 00:00 UTC, and fractional days are allowed, so DayOfYear(153.5) is noon on June 1st 2020.

A plain number is deliberately not accepted as a time; instead use a Unitful time such as 100u"d" to mean the time elapsed since T0.

StandardHaloModelsPP.T0 — Constant

June 1st: the time at which the Earth's orbital velocity $\vec{v}_{ES}$ is most closely aligned with the Sun's $\vec{v}_\odot$, so the Earth's galactic speed peaks, for the reference-value VLSR. Value: 2020-06-01T03:50:00

T0 is the time origin for every model (e.g. of Unitful times), so it does not move with a model's LSR speed. For the provided preset models the alignment stays within a day of it (half a day earlier for SHMlegacy).

vE(m::HaloModel, t::TimeLike)
VE(m::HaloModel, t::TimeLike)

The Earth's galactic velocity and speed at t, for model m: boosted with the model's own LSR speed m.VLSR (by default its v₀) rather than the reference-value VLSR.

If m does not contain uncertainties then ephemeris uncertainties are not added to the result (for performance reasons), otherwise uncertainties will be included.

Unqualified vE(t),VE(t) use the reference-value VLSR, and always carry uncertainties.
